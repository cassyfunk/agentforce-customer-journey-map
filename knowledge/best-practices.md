# Customer Journey Map — Best Practices

**Source:** Hanover Research — Customer Journey Mapping Toolkit (2024)

---

## Why Journey Maps Matter

Nearly 90% of companies that use journey maps see increases in customer satisfaction, lower churn, fewer customer complaints, and a higher NPS.

> "Accurately mapping the customer's journey requires in-depth research and data analysis to identify and evaluate the various elements of the customer journey. In addition to the journey itself, businesses also need insight into customers' thoughts and experiences, an understanding of what factors are helping or hindering them on the path to a purchase, and a clear set of KPIs to measure success against."

### Six business uses for a journey map

1. **Improve Products or Services** — Journey insights reveal which aspects delight or fall short, uncovering opportunities for new features and offerings.
2. **Optimize Resource Allocation** — 90%+ of companies use journey data to determine ROI of marketing/advertising; 76% say it increases investment ROI.
3. **Drive Sales** — Enables tailored outreach aligned to where the buyer is in the journey.
4. **Increase Customer Satisfaction** — 90%+ of companies say journey data has helped increase satisfaction and develop loyalty.
5. **Identify Gaps in Service and Communication** — Reveals weaknesses in sales touchpoints, messaging, products, support channels.
6. **Differentiate from Competitors** — Understanding customer actions and what influences purchase/retention decisions enables stronger offerings.

## Preliminary Step: Segment Your Audiences

> "Trying to build one customer journey map across different products, customer needs, industries, and more, will cause the map to be too generalized and provide limited insights."

Segment customers using multiple factors — demographic, behavioral, and psychographic — not just a single attribute like industry or role. One segment = one map.

**For Agentforce:** consider segments such as:
- Admin-led deployment (IT/Ops-owned)
- Business-led deployment (Sales/Service team-owned)
- SI/partner-led deployment

## The Five Journey Phases (Hanover Framework)

> "For each stage you should identify the specific actions a customer takes (both online and offline), the touchpoints and interactions a customer has with your company, customer insights into their motivations and behaviors, and performance metrics that are measurable indicators of success."

### Phase 1: Awareness
Customer has realized they have a problem/need and is aware your company could solve it.
- **Customer Action:** Visits website, reads blog, interacts on social media
- **Touchpoints:** Media coverage, website, online reviews, advertisements, word of mouth
- **KPIs:** Brand awareness, customer motivations/preferences, web traffic, search rankings, social media reach, newsletter sign-ups
- **Customer Insights to gather:** What are their product needs? What triggered the need? Is there a specific solution in mind? Are they aware of our brand?

### Phase 2: Consideration
Buyers are evaluating options — reading reviews, watching tutorials, viewing case studies.
- **Customer Action:** Compares and evaluates product features
- **Touchpoints:** Website, chatbot conversations, thought leadership, product collateral, demo videos, email
- **KPIs:** Website activity, engagement data, product ratings, customer research, lead acquisition
- **Customer Insights to gather:** What research was conducted? What brands/channels are being considered? What features do they respond to? What messaging resonates? What drives the decision?

### Phase 3: Purchase
Customer has gathered enough information; actively evaluating a purchase. May purchase from you, a competitor, or not at all.
- **Customer Action:** Contacts customer service, makes an order
- **Touchpoints:** Sales conversations, demos, product trials
- **KPIs:** Purchases/POS data, cart abandonment, purchase price, meeting/demo requests, sales cycle length
- **Customer Insights to gather:** Where did the purchase happen? What was the final decision? Budget vs. actual spend? What considerations factored in? Who was involved? What pain points occurred?

### Phase 4: Retention
Customer is using the product and wants to validate they made the right decision.
- **Customer Action:** Using product, considering whether to stay vs. switch to a lower-price competitor
- **Touchpoints:** Service interactions, self-service activities, product usage
- **KPIs:** Retention rate, churn rate, customer lifetime value, service call ratings, customer satisfaction
- **Customer Insights to gather:** Are customers satisfied? How do they evaluate the offering? What drives satisfaction? Will/when will they purchase again? Will they increase or decrease budget?

### Phase 5: Loyalty
Customer has a favorable view of brand/product value but is not fully invested — may have repurchased or expanded to additional services, but is still open to other brands.
- **Customer Action:** Repurchases and is willing to provide a customer quote/reference
- **Touchpoints:** Renewal conversations, product usage, feedback surveys
- **KPIs:** Customer satisfaction, NPS, renewal rate, purchase frequency, brand engagement, product usage
- **Customer Insights to gather:** How likely are they to recommend? Why likely or unlikely? What elements do they value most? What makes us competitive? What additional support/features do they need?

## Data to Inform Your Maps

### Customer Data (already have)
- Website activity
- Purchase data
- Sales interactions
- Customer behavior data
- Customer firmographic/demographic data
- Customer reviews
- Usage data

### Market Research (need to gather)
- **Surveys:** Assess knowledge/perception of brand, needs, preferences, reported behaviors
- **Interviews and Focus Groups:** Direct feedback on specific points of the buying and user experience; helps develop solutions addressing pain points
- **Third-party data:** Competitive intelligence, market benchmarks

## Leveraging Your Map: Developing Data-Driven Strategies

### Identify Problem Areas
Focus on common exit points and blocks. Watch for:
- Low brand awareness or perception leading to low leads and sales conversations
- Missing or unsupported communication touchpoints leading to dropped or stalled cycles
- Confusing or misaligned value propositions that turn customers toward competitors

### Assess Opportunities
Identify touchpoints, actions, and stages that are successful or ripe for improvement:
- Positive brand and product reviews → increased interest and brand value
- Untapped product value → promote as competitive advantage
- High satisfaction and product evaluations → support renewals and increased sales

---

## Updating Your Map

> "93% of companies with customer journey maps have plans to refresh their maps with new data."

Maps should be updated when:
- New technology or competitive dynamics shift the landscape
- New products or features change the customer path
- Target customer profile or sales positioning changes
- Behaviors at key touchpoints shift measurably

**For Agentforce:** plan to refresh after each major release drop, after large customer research studies, and when CS support top-asks shift significantly.

## What a Good Map Includes (Checklist)

- [ ] One map per customer segment — not one generic map for all customers
- [ ] Customer perspective at every stage, not company perspective
- [ ] Both positive moments AND pain points — the full emotional arc
- [ ] Customer Actions: what the customer is actually doing at each stage
- [ ] Touchpoints: every channel and interaction
- [ ] Thoughts / Customer Insights: motivations, questions, decision factors
- [ ] Feelings / Sentiment: emotional state (drives the emotion curve)
- [ ] Pain points: friction, confusion, failure moments
- [ ] Opportunities: where to improve or differentiate
- [ ] KPIs: measurable indicators tied to each stage
- [ ] Hidden friction points: problems that emerge *between* interactions

## Stage-Specific Guidance (Agentforce Application)

### SKU Provisioned / Purchase
- Focus on the moment after the sale — the "what now?" gap is the #1 hidden friction point
- Map the handoff between sales and implementation/CSM explicitly
- Customer insight questions: Where did purchase happen? Who was involved? What pain points at purchase?
- KPI: Time from contract signed to first login

### Agent Created
- This is the highest-friction stage — setup complexity is the #1 barrier
- Note every field/decision a user must make; each one is a potential drop-off
- Customer insight questions: Is there a specific use case in mind? What's blocking progress?
- KPI: Time to first agent published; % completing setup within 30 days

### Agent Being Used
- Capture both admin experience (monitoring) and end-user experience (conversations)
- First-session success rate is the key metric — a bad first session kills adoption
- Customer insight questions: Are they satisfied? What's driving satisfaction or dissatisfaction?
- KPI: CSAT on first 10 conversations; session completion rate

### Scaled Agent Usage
- Governance, change management, and org-wide adoption are the primary themes
- Map the internal champion's journey — they are the vehicle for scale
- Customer insight questions: How likely to recommend? What additional support is needed?
- KPI: Active agents per org; % of target users engaging weekly; NPS; renewal rate
